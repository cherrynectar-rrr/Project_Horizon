# Life Operations — Nutrition & Recovery

Status: Approved — Pending Specialist Initialization
Authorized by: Horizon Core
Start date: 2026-09-03

## Mission

Build a healthy, economical, low-friction and sustainable daily-life system that supports Project Horizon's academic, engineering and research work rather than becoming another high-maintenance project.

The initial focus is **Nutrition & Recovery**:

- everyday diet structure;
- campus / low-budget meal choices;
- protein, vegetables, fruit, staple foods and hydration at a practical level;
- meal timing when it materially affects study, training or sleep;
- supplements only when evidence, likely benefit, cost and safety justify them;
- caffeine and other common performance-related habits;
- simple food shopping / stocking / meal-prep decisions when useful;
- recovery-supporting routines that interact strongly with nutrition, especially sleep and exercise;
- lightweight budget coordination with Personal Finance when materially relevant.

## Role

This is a **Life Operations / Maintenance** module, not a new technical main line and not a bodybuilding, biohacking or supplement-collection project.

Its purpose is to reduce friction and improve stable energy, health support and long-term sustainability.

It should consume very little ongoing attention once a workable baseline exists.

## Operating Principles

1. **Food first, supplements second.**
   Supplements should solve a defined problem or provide a well-supported low-risk benefit; they are not the foundation of the system.

2. **Minimum effective complexity.**
   Prefer a small number of repeatable meal patterns over constant optimization, tracking or novelty.

3. **Evidence over marketing.**
   Supplement decisions should distinguish strong evidence, conditional evidence, uncertain benefit and marketing claims.

4. **Cost matters.**
   Evaluate benefit per yuan as well as biological plausibility. A costly supplement that provides marginal benefit should not displace better food, sleep, exercise, study resources or financial reserves.

5. **No pseudo-precision.**
   Do not require calorie, macro, micronutrient or biomarker tracking unless it answers a real question. Rough meal templates and periodic checks are preferred when sufficient.

6. **Health and safety boundary.**
   Medical symptoms, diagnosed conditions, medication interactions, abnormal laboratory values, eating disorders, significant unintended weight change, high-dose supplementation or other clinically meaningful issues are outside ordinary Life Operations optimization and should be handled with appropriate healthcare guidance.

7. **Current evidence when mutable.**
   Supplement safety guidance, upper intake limits, regulatory status and clinically relevant recommendations must be checked from current reliable sources when they affect a decision.

8. **Support Horizon rather than compete with it.**
   This module must not become a second daily curriculum. Its ideal end state is a boring system that works automatically.

## Initial Baseline — Nutrition & Recovery v1

The first Specialist session should build a practical baseline around the user's real environment rather than a generic ideal diet.

Minimum questions / evidence domains:

- typical weekly meal environment: canteen, takeout, dorm food, cooking access;
- approximate food budget and convenience constraints;
- commonly eaten foods and obvious gaps;
- study schedule and meal-timing friction;
- exercise pattern if relevant;
- current supplements, if any;
- major goals such as stable energy, adequate nutrition, convenience, cost control or training support;
- foods / patterns the user strongly dislikes or cannot realistically sustain.

Do not collect sensitive medical information unless the user voluntarily raises it and it is necessary to answer a specific health question.

## Baseline Output

Nutrition & Recovery v1 should ideally produce:

1. **Daily meal architecture** — a few simple meal rules rather than a rigid menu.
2. **Campus decision rules** — how to choose reasonably good meals under ordinary cafeteria / takeout conditions.
3. **Protein / produce / hydration floor** — simple practical targets only if useful.
4. **Supplement table** — `use / conditional / skip / unresolved`, with reason, dose range only when appropriate and current evidence supports it, cost and safety notes.
5. **Low-friction shopping list** — only if the user actually keeps food in a dorm / apartment.
6. **Recovery guardrails** — a small number of nutrition / caffeine / sleep interactions worth protecting.
7. **Monthly review** — very short, focused on whether the system is working rather than generating data for its own sake.

## Example Supplement Decision Filter

For every proposed supplement, ask:

1. What specific problem or goal is it addressing?
2. Is the expected benefit meaningful for this user context?
3. How strong is the evidence?
4. Can food or a simpler habit solve the same problem?
5. What are the dose, side effects, interactions and quality-control concerns?
6. What does it cost per month?
7. What would we stop buying or doing to make room for it?

If these questions do not produce a convincing answer, default to **skip**.

## Relationship to Other Horizon Areas

### Personal Finance

Life Operations owns nutrition / recovery choices. Personal Finance owns the broader cash-flow and savings system. Coordinate only when food or supplement decisions materially affect the monthly budget.

### Academic / Technical Work

Life Operations may protect energy, sleep and daily reliability, but it does not create study curricula or technical priorities.

### Exercise

Exercise can be considered where it materially interacts with nutrition and recovery. A separate full fitness line should not be created unless later evidence shows a real need.

## First Gate

**Nutrition & Recovery Baseline v1**

Pass when the user has a realistic, affordable and low-maintenance food / supplement system that can be followed for several weeks without excessive tracking or attention.

The first version should favor execution over theoretical completeness.
