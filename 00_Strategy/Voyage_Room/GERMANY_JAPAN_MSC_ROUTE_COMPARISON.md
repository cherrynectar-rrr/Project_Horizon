# Germany vs Japan MSc Route Comparison v1

Last Updated: 2026-08-28
Owner: Voyage Room
Status: Strategic Research / Advisory
Decision Authority: Project Control

## Purpose

Compare Germany and Japan as serious 2029 MSc + early-career routes for Project Horizon under the current constraints: EIE background, control/robotics/intelligent-systems interest, approximately RMB 100,000/year all-in planning band, normal 2029 graduation, TOEFL as common English asset, and no simultaneous high-intensity German + Japanese language lines.

## Executive Judgment

Both routes remain strategically viable, but they optimize different things.

- **Germany currently has the stronger long-horizon life/career/settlement profile** if the user can absorb the German-language burden and selects a financially viable programme. It offers a broader European labour market context, faster and more predictable post-study settlement pathways, higher nominal engineering pay, and substantially lower average annual working hours.
- **Japan currently has the stronger admission-language efficiency and robotics/manufacturing research fit** through English-taught national-university programmes such as Science Tokyo IGP(C). It can preserve Japan as a serious route without requiring Japanese for admission, and MEXT creates unusually strong funding upside.
- **TUM specifically is not representative of Germany's cheapest route.** Its current non-EU tuition for Electrical and Computer Engineering is EUR 6,000/semester and Munich living costs are high. Other German public universities can be materially cheaper.
- **Science Tokyo specifically is not representative of all Japan admissions.** It is unusually attractive because IGP(C) is English-taught and has no Japanese admission requirement; other Japanese programmes may require written/oral examinations, supervisor consent, GRE or different procedures.

Current recommendation: **keep both routes open, but do not run both local-language tracks at high intensity. Build common assets now and set a Germany–Japan Strategic Gate before German escalates into B2/C1/TestDaF-level workload.**

## Cost Interpretation

### Germany

Do not equate Germany with TUM.

- TUM Electrical and Computer Engineering currently charges non-EU students EUR 6,000 per semester.
- Munich student living costs are currently around EUR 1,200+ per month and official 2026 student-support material cites roughly EUR 1,250–1,300 average monthly living costs.
- Therefore **TUM is currently above Horizon's preferred RMB 100k/year band without a waiver/scholarship or unusually low living costs**.
- Across Germany, DAAD currently gives EUR 900–1,200/month as a common living-cost range; many public master's programmes still have no tuition, though Baden-Württemberg and some Bavarian institutions/programmes charge non-EU tuition.

### Japan

- Official Study in Japan data currently gives approximately JPY 820,000 first-year academic fees for a national graduate school on average.
- Science Tokyo currently charges JPY 282,000 enrollment + JPY 635,400 annual tuition + JPY 30,000 application fee.
- Official international-student living-cost average is JPY 105,000/month excluding study costs; Tokyo housing is above the national average.
- Regional national universities can be materially cheaper in living cost than Tokyo.

Interpretation: **Japan has a cleaner cluster of nationally strong programmes around the current affordability band; Germany has greater cost dispersion. TUM is currently expensive, while other public German routes may undercut Japan.**

## Admissions Risk

### Germany risk profile

Strengths:
- rule-driven and comparatively legible;
- no need to persuade a specific professor before many taught MSc applications;
- application can be diversified across programmes.

Risks:
- hard curricular prerequisites can reject a candidate even with good GPA;
- APS/VPD/document requirements create process complexity;
- German-language proof is a real multi-year workload for the current TUM benchmark;
- TUM's current tuition weakens affordability.

### Japan risk profile

Strengths:
- strong English-track routes can remove Japanese as an admission gate;
- research/project evidence can directly strengthen professor/lab matching;
- good fit for candidates who develop a concrete research interest.

Risks:
- lab/supervisor fit creates higher variance and dependence on a small number of people;
- programmes differ sharply in exam format, GRE, interview, physical attendance and timing;
- Japanese can be unnecessary for admission but important for later employment, creating a delayed language bill;
- Japanese new-graduate recruiting starts early and follows a distinctive domestic process.

## Career and Capital Formation

### Germany

Current official Federal Employment Agency data shows high full-time median gross pay for electrical engineering / automation / technical cybernetics occupations. These are all-age medians, not new-graduate salaries, but they indicate a strong engineering compensation market.

Germany's 2025 labour market weakened compared with prior years; the Federal Employment Agency no longer classifies mechatronics/energy/electrical engineering as general shortage occupations and places several in monitoring. Therefore the route should not be sold as “automatic engineer shortage = guaranteed job.”

Still, Germany has three structural advantages for Horizon's freedom objective:

1. higher nominal engineering compensation;
2. lower average annual working hours;
3. a comparatively fast and legible settlement route for graduates and Blue Card holders.

### Japan

Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare job-tag data currently shows strong demand in industrial-robot development (job-offer ratio above 3 in current data) and substantial mature-career pay in electrical/robotics occupations. This confirms real labour-market depth in areas relevant to Horizon.

However, Japanese employment has a larger local-language and recruiting-process dependence. JASSO explicitly describes a unique new-graduate hiring system and advises international students to prepare Japanese proficiency and recruiting activities while still enrolled.

Japan can still be excellent for technical depth and a manufacturing/robotics career, but **the current default expectation should be that serious long-term Japan employment eventually requires meaningful Japanese, even if the master's admission did not.**

## Settlement and Life Flexibility

### Germany

Current rules:
- up to 18 months after graduation to seek qualified employment;
- German-university graduates can apply for a settlement permit after more than two years in skilled employment if requirements are met;
- EU Blue Card holders can reach settlement after 27 months with A1 German, or 21 months with B1, if other requirements are met.

This is a very strong fit with Horizon's long-term freedom objective because the path from student to durable residence is comparatively explicit.

### Japan

Current rules:
- normally up to one year of post-graduation job hunting under Designated Activities, with certain further-extension routes;
- permanent residence in principle normally requires 10 years of residence including at least 5 years on a qualifying work/residence status;
- Highly Skilled Professional points can reduce this to 3 years at 70 points or 1 year at 80 points under current rules.

Japan's fast path can therefore be excellent for a high-scoring technical professional, but the default non-HSP path is materially longer and less automatic than Germany's graduate route.

## Work / Life Environment

Use national averages only as directional evidence, not as a promise about a particular employer.

OECD 2024 average annual hours actually worked:
- Germany: 1,334 hours
- Japan: 1,617 hours

Statutory annual paid leave:
- Germany: minimum 20 days/year for a 5-day work week; many collective agreements provide more.
- Japan: normally 10 days after six months of qualifying employment, rising with tenure to 20 days after 6.5 years.

This gives Germany a meaningful structural edge for the user's stated objective of eventually having freedom over how to live rather than maximizing career prestige alone.

## Research-Direction Fit

If future evidence shows the user's strongest interest becomes:

- **control theory / industrial automation / cyber-physical systems / automotive control / broad EU engineering** → Germany gains weight;
- **robotics hardware / mechatronics / embodied systems / sensors / industrial robotics / electronics manufacturing integration** → Japan gains weight;
- **AI-heavy software / pure ML** → neither country should be selected solely from today's robotics narrative; re-open the broader international pool.

Do not force the country decision before the research-direction evidence exists.

## Language Decision

Current strategy:

- keep English/TOEFL as the common asset;
- German can continue at low-cost foundation level while Germany remains the benchmark;
- do **not** activate Japanese as a second serious language line now;
- before committing to B2/C1/TestDaF-level German intensity, run a Germany–Japan Strategic Gate based on updated school/lab pool, research interests, cost, employment and life preference;
- if Japan becomes the clear primary route, German should be reduced before Japanese is escalated.

## Current Conditional Recommendation

If forced to choose **today**:

- choose **Germany** if the main objective is: long-term settlement, work-life balance, higher engineering income, European mobility, and the user is willing to pay the German-language cost;
- choose **Japan** if the main objective is: English-accessible top robotics/control research, closer geography, strong manufacturing/robotics ecosystem, and lower admission-language burden, while accepting that Japanese likely becomes important for long-term employment.

For Project Horizon today: **do not choose yet. Preserve both through common assets and set a formal decision gate before high-intensity local-language investment.**
