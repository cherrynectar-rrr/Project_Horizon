# MSc Application Exam Strategy — TUM-Primary, Multi-School Coverage

Last Updated: 2026-08-22
Owner: Voyage Room
Status: Strategic recommendation; advisory only

## Purpose

Design the smallest reusable exam-preparation stack that preserves the current QS Top-100 affordable robotics/automation MSc pool while keeping TUM as the user's primary target/benchmark.

This document does not activate language/GRE execution by itself. Project Control owns workload allocation.

## User-Confirmed Position

- Preserve the broader target pool rather than collapsing to TUM only.
- TUM remains the primary target/benchmark.
- Preparation should maximize cross-school reuse and avoid sitting multiple redundant language tests.

## Recommended Minimum Exam Stack

### 1. English — one exam only

Preferred common exam: **IELTS Academic**.

Planning target: **Overall 7.0, ideally no component below 6.5.**

This is a Horizon planning target, not a claim that every programme requires 7.0. Current 2026 minima are generally lower and vary by programme. The purpose of 7.0/6.5 is to create cross-school margin and keep one score usable across most of the pool.

Current examples:
- TUM RCI: English proof required; TUM's general English certificate page currently accepts IELTS Academic 6.5 / TOEFL iBT 88 for English-language requirements.
- University of Osaka Engineering Science 21st Century: IELTS 6.0 / TOEFL iBT 79 / TOEIC L&R 785 or equivalent.
- KAIST international graduate admission: IELTS 6.5 / TOEFL iBT 83 / TOEIC 720 / TEPS equivalent.
- Politecnico di Milano: IELTS 6.0 (or accepted TOEFL/other certificates).
- Seoul National University international graduate admission: IELTS Academic 6.0 / TOEFL iBT 80 / TEPS 269, or qualifying Korean proof.
- Kyoto Informatics International Program: TOEFL/TOEIC/IELTS scores are used as an admissions scoring component rather than merely a pass/fail certificate.
- KTH Systems, Control and Robotics: IELTS 6.5 or equivalent English 6 requirement.
- University of Tokyo IME: TOEFL preferred; IELTS accepted.
- Paris-Saclay M1 International Track in Electrical Engineering: certificate of English level is compulsory for non-English speakers; programme page does not publish one universal numerical minimum.

Do not take both IELTS and TOEFL unless a later target programme creates a real incompatibility.

### 2. German — primary TUM-specific long-horizon requirement

Primary TUM route: **M.Sc. Electrical and Computer Engineering -> Automation and Robotics**.

Current 2026 requirement: German proficiency must be proved by the application deadline; programme is mainly taught in German.

Preferred certificate route:
- **TestDaF: TDN 4 in all four sections**, or
- **telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule: pass**.

Horizon should treat the practical target as university-level B2-to-C1 competence, not certificate-only cramming.

Because TUM is the primary target and German takes much longer to build than GRE, German is the unique requirement that deserves the earliest strategic runway if Project Control approves language activation.

### 3. GRE General — preserve RCI / University of Tokyo upside, but prepare later

GRE is **not currently required for TUM ECE**.

It is currently required/relevant for:
- TUM Robotics, Cognition, Intelligence (RCI) for applicants whose first degree is from China: Quantitative 164 minimum, Analytical Writing 4.0 minimum, no Verbal minimum.
- University of Tokyo IME: GRE General required.
- Politecnico di Milano: GRE is optional and may strengthen an engineering application; no programme-wide minimum is currently set.

Recommended Horizon planning target if GRE remains necessary in the final pool:
- Quantitative: **166+**
- Analytical Writing: **4.0+**
- Verbal: **150+** as a sensible competitiveness target, not a TUM RCI formal minimum.

Rationale: this clears current TUM RCI minima with margin and produces a usable score for UTokyo IME without optimizing solely for one programme.

GRE should be prepared after English is sufficiently stable and only if RCI / UTokyo or another GRE-requiring target remains alive close enough to the application cycle.

## Programme-Specific Oral / Technical Screening

This is not one standardized exam, but a common technical interview package can cover several schools.

Current examples:
- TUM ECE: applicants may be invited to a ~20-minute aptitude-assessment interview depending on Step-1 score.
- University of Osaka Engineering Science 21st Century: overseas applicants are screened by documents + internet interview.
- Kyoto Intelligence Science and Technology International Program: current selection includes a 5-minute presentation and English Q&A on academic knowledge and proposed research, with English-test score contributing separately.
- University of Tokyo IME: documents plus possible online examinations/interviews.
- Science Tokyo IGP: English ability is currently assessed through interviews/examinations/communication; external English scores are generally no longer a universal requirement unless scholarship/supervisor requests them.

Common technical-interview preparation base:
- higher mathematics: calculus, linear algebra, probability;
- circuits / analog / digital electronics fundamentals;
- signals and systems + DSP;
- control fundamentals;
- algorithms and data structures;
- C/C++ / Python and project implementation;
- embedded systems / sensors when relevant;
- ability to explain bachelor courses from the transcript;
- research interests, research proposal and project evidence.

This should be built through the normal undergraduate/Horizon capability route rather than as a separate exam-cramming curriculum.

## Germany Administrative Gate — APS

APS is not a normal university admission test but remains a Germany-specific gate for applicants with Chinese university credentials.

Current 2026 China procedure requires credential verification plus either APS interview or TestAS depending on the applicable procedure. APS interview currently contains approximately 20 minutes written preparation + up to ~25 minutes oral discussion focused on whether the applicant genuinely studied and understands courses shown on the Chinese transcript. It is not primarily a language exam.

Because APS/TestAS procedures have recently changed, Horizon must re-check the current rule before the actual application cycle rather than committing to a 2026 path now.

## Languages Not Worth Activating for Admission Right Now

For the currently selected English-track routes, there is no present need to add entrance-exam preparation for:
- Japanese;
- Korean;
- French;
- Italian.

These languages may later have strong life/internship/employment value, but they should not be opened now merely for MSc admission while TUM remains the main target.

## Current Priority Order — Strategic, Not Yet an Execution Schedule

1. **German** — longest runway; uniquely important because TUM is primary.
2. **English / IELTS Academic** — universal application infrastructure across almost the entire pool.
3. **Technical foundations + interview communication** — built through GPA/coursework/projects rather than a separate test track.
4. **GRE General** — later, conditional on RCI / UTokyo / another GRE target remaining in the final pool.
5. **APS preparation** — Germany-specific and timed to the actual application/APS cycle.

## Important Guardrail

Do not translate this stack into simultaneous high-intensity German + IELTS + GRE preparation during the current undergraduate stage.

The correct principle is:

> preserve all required future exits now; activate each standardized test only when its lead time and application deadline justify the opportunity cost.
