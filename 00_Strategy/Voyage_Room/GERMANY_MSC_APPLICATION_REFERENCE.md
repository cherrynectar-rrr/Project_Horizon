## 1. Overall Assessment

### Verdict

**Useful and generally high-quality as a 2026 snapshot, but not authoritative and not complete enough to function as Horizon's Germany MSc decision system by itself.**

The guide is strongest on:

- application sequence;
- APS / uni-assist / VPD concepts;
- language-certificate planning;
- program-by-program deadline awareness;
- document preparation;
- examples showing that TUM, RWTH and TU Berlin differ sharply;
- warning against relying on generic internet rules instead of programme pages.

It is weaker or incomplete on:

- unstable APS TestAS-vs-interview policy;
- overly broad heuristics such as a general `2.5` grade threshold;
- non-universal Chinese-credit → ECTS conversion;
- the meaning of `zulassungsfrei` / no-NC;
- full financial planning and housing risk;
- scholarships and funding strategy;
- post-arrival residence administration;
- student work / internships / Werkstudent rules;
- graduation → job search → EU Blue Card → permanent-residence route;
- Horizon-specific prerequisite auditing for Robotics / Intelligent Systems / Embodied AI;
- how to turn undergraduate projects, research, thesis and internships into application evidence.

Use information in this order:

## 2. Claims Verified Against Official Sources

### 2.1 Tuition: mostly correct, with programme-level checking required

Verified from DAAD and TUM:

- Most state universities generally do not charge tuition for most Bachelor's and Master's programmes.
- Baden-Württemberg generally charges non-EU students EUR 1,500 per semester, subject to exceptions.
- Bavaria allows individual universities to charge non-EU tuition; policies differ by institution.
- TUM normally charges non-EU students EUR 4,000 or EUR 6,000 per semester for Master's programmes, plus the regular semester fee.

Official sources:

- DAAD — Costs of education and living: https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/living-in-germany/finances/
- TUM — Tuition Fees for Students from Non-EU Countries: https://www.tum.de/en/studies/fees/tuition

**Horizon rule:** never infer affordability from the label “German public university”. Compare realistic all-in cost per programme and city.

### 2.2 uni-assist fees and VPD timing: verified

Verified from uni-assist:

- First chosen course in one semester: EUR 75.
- Each additional chosen course in the same semester: EUR 30.
- Same fee system applies to VPD and standard procedures.
- VPD processing usually takes 4–6 weeks.
- A uni-assist VPD is generally valid for one year, but the receiving university can have its own acceptance rules.
- A VPD request is not the same as applying to the university; the university application must still be completed when required.

Official sources:

- https://www.uni-assist.de/en/how-to-apply/pay-all-fees/handling-fees/
- https://www.uni-assist.de/en/how-to-apply/plan-your-application/vpd/

Important TUM-specific nuance:

- TUM states that its VPD procedure has university-specific rules.
- TUM currently accepts its VPD for up to three years if the relevant qualification and admission requirements have not changed.
- TUM does not accept a VPD issued for another university.

Official source:

- https://www.tum.de/en/studies/application/application-info-portal/uni-assist/

### 2.3 China / APS requirement: core idea verified

For applicants with Chinese educational qualifications, APS is a central formal requirement for many German university applications and visa procedures. uni-assist's China page requires the original digitally verifiable APS certificate where applicable.

Official sources:

- APS: https://www.aps.org.cn/zh/verfahren-und-services-deutschland/chinaverfahren
- uni-assist China: https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/cn/

APS fees verified as of 2026:

- General China procedure interview: RMB 2,500.
- TestAS route: RMB 2,500.

Official source:

- https://www.aps.org.cn/zh/verfahren-und-services-deutschland/entgelte

### 2.4 TUM Informatics example: substantially verified

Current official TUM information confirms:

- M.Sc. Informatics: 120 ECTS, four semesters, English, Garching.
- Winter application period: 1 February–31 May.
- Summer application period: 1 October–30 November.
- Admission uses Master's aptitude assessment.
- Applicants with a bachelor's degree outside Germany need a VPD.
- Applicants with a degree from China currently need GRE General scores for this programme; the current Informatics page lists Quantitative 164 and Analytical Writing 4.0, while Verbal is no longer counted.
- APS is required for applicants with an undergraduate degree from China.
- The application includes curricular analysis, CV, statement of reasons and a scientific essay.

Official sources:

- https://www.tum.de/en/studies/degree-programs/detail/informatics-master-of-science-msc
- https://www.cit.tum.de/en/cit/studies/degree-programs/master-informatics/

**Important supplement not emphasized enough in the PDF:** TUM's current Informatics page explicitly states that the scientific essay and statement of reasons must be the applicant's own work and may be checked for authorship / unauthorized assistance. Future Horizon application support must respect programme-specific authorship rules.

### 2.5 TUM English minimums: verified as central TUM standards, but programme rules still govern

TUM currently recognizes, among other options:

- TOEFL iBT: 88.
- IELTS Academic: 6.5 overall.
- PTE Academic: 65.

TUM also states that the corresponding score under the new TOEFL 1–6 scoring system is accepted.

Official source:

- https://www.tum.de/en/studies/application/application-info-portal/admission-requirements/language-certificates

ETS independently confirms that TOEFL moved to a 1–6 scale from 21 January 2026, with a two-year transition period showing comparable legacy scores.

Official source:

- https://www.ets.org/toefl/institutions/ibt/score-scale-update.html

### 2.6 RWTH General Mechanical Engineering example: core profile verified

RWTH currently lists Allgemeiner Maschinenbau M.Sc. as:

- 90 ECTS;
- three semesters;
- German-taught;
- winter and summer entry;
- open admission / no NC;
- subject-specific prior-knowledge requirements governed by the examination regulations.

Official source:

- https://www.rwth-aachen.de/cms/root/Studium/Vor-dem-Studium/Studiengaenge/Liste-Aktuelle-Studiengaenge/Studiengangbeschreibung/~bkwg/Allgemeiner-Maschinenbau-M-Sc-/

RWTH's general rules for applicants from non-EU countries whose Master's entrance qualification was not obtained in Germany currently use final deadlines of:

- 1 March for winter semester;
- 1 September for summer semester.

Official legal / university source:

- RWTH enrolment regulation / official admissions material, §13(3)(b).

### 2.7 TU Berlin Electrical Engineering example: key prerequisites verified

TU Berlin currently lists Electrical Engineering M.Sc. as:

- 120 ECTS;
- four semesters;
- German-taught;
- summer and winter start;
- open admission;
- first degree in Electrical Engineering or a closely related subject;
- normally at least 27 CP mathematics / natural sciences;
- at least 54 CP electrical engineering;
- at least 6 CP computer science;
- additional English proof at CEFR B2 because some classes are taught in English.

Official source:

- https://www.tu.berlin/en/studying/study-programs/all-programs-offered/study-course/electrical-engineering-m-sc

### 2.8 Student financial proof: amount verified as a 2026 snapshot

Official German sources currently use EUR 992/month, i.e. EUR 11,904 for twelve months, as a relevant study-visa funding reference amount for 2026.

Official sources:

- DAAD: https://www.daad.de/en/studying-in-germany/living-in-germany/finances/
- Make-it-in-Germany: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/studying
- Federal Foreign Office blocked-account guidance: https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/sperrkonto/388600

**Horizon rule:** this amount is a visa-proof floor, not a realistic all-in living budget for every city. Munich and other expensive cities can require materially more.

### 2.9 Student work rights: important missing supplement, officially verified

As of 2026, third-country international students may generally work:

- up to 140 full days or 280 half-days per year; or
- alternatively up to 20 hours per week during the lecture period under the current rules.

Student auxiliary / university-related work has special treatment.

Official source:

- https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/study-vocational-training/studies-in-germany/work

This matters strategically for Werkstudent, HiWi, internships, industry exposure and future employment, and should be part of Horizon's Germany route even though the PDF focuses mainly on admission.

### 2.10 Post-graduation route: important missing supplement, officially verified

After completing a German degree, international graduates may currently obtain a residence permit for up to 18 months to seek qualified employment; they may work in any occupation while searching.

Official source:

- https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/studying

For Project Horizon, the preferred German residence strategy, if the route becomes real and the job qualifies, is currently:

`German MSc → qualified engineering job → EU Blue Card → permanent-residence route`

This is a strategic preference, not a reason to accept a materially worse job merely to satisfy a residence category.

## 3. Claims That Need Caution or Correction

### 3.1 APS TestAS vs interview — do NOT hard-code the PDF rule

This is the most important caution.

The PDF correctly notices that official APS pages are not perfectly aligned, but its simplified takeaway can still become stale quickly.

Current APS information in August 2026 shows a **pilot / exam-specific TestAS arrangement**. One current adjustment notice for the 24 October 2026 TestAS permits eligible undergraduate students, including students in the 8th semester of a four-year bachelor's or 10th semester of a five-year bachelor's, if they have not previously taken the China-procedure TestAS or interview. Other APS general / interview pages describe the boundary differently.

Official sources:

- https://www.aps.org.cn/zh/verfahren-und-services-deutschland/chinaverfahren
- https://www.aps.org.cn/zh/verfahren-und-services-deutschland/chinaverfahren/testas
- APS 2026 TestAS adjustment / FAQ pages.

**Horizon rule:** when APS becomes actionable, determine the route from the exact current APS notice for that application cycle. Never preserve “undergraduate = TestAS, graduate = interview” as a permanent rule.

### 3.2 “German grade 2.5 is the common Master's threshold” — heuristic only

There is no single national German Master's admission threshold of 2.5.

Programmes vary substantially:

- some use aptitude scoring;
- some use programme-specific minimum grades;
- some focus primarily on curricular equivalence;
- some are open admission but still require complete subject prerequisites;
- some have NC / capacity limits.

**Horizon rule:** 2.5 may be useful as a rough internet heuristic, but may not be used to decide whether the user “can apply”. Use the actual programme regulations.

### 3.3 Chinese credits → ECTS — PDF self-check method is too simplified

The PDF's suggestion to estimate ECTS from course hours can help rough thinking, but there is **no universal Germany-wide conversion formula that Horizon should treat as authoritative**.

TUM examples show that programmes may use their own conversion factors and individual assessment based on workload, programme duration and course structure. One TUM programme currently gives China only as an approximate range of 1 Chinese credit ≈ 1.3–1.7 ECTS, while other TUM curricular-analysis tools calculate a programme-specific factor.

Official examples:

- https://www.sot.tum.de/en/sot/studium/ai-in-society/application/
- TUM curricular-analysis / ECTS conversion instructions.

**Horizon rule:** preserve original Chinese credits, contact hours, course descriptions and official syllabus / training-plan evidence. Perform conversion using the target programme's explicit method when one exists.

### 3.4 `zulassungsfrei` / no NC does not mean “automatic admission with no further review”

No NC means there is no capacity-based admission restriction of that type. It does **not** eliminate:

- degree-background requirements;
- curricular prerequisites;
- language requirements;
- document deadlines;
- programme-specific examination rules;
- possible special assessment where regulations permit it.

RWTH's general rules explicitly still require formal and subject-specific suitability review for open-admission Master's applicants.

**Horizon rule:** translate `zulassungsfrei` as “no NC / no quota restriction”, not “guaranteed admission”.

### 3.5 “Application costs about RMB 10,000” — scenario estimate, not a planning constant

This depends on:

- APS route;
- IELTS / TOEFL / TestDaF / GRE retakes;
- number of uni-assist applications;
- notarization / translation / Apostille needs;
- university-specific fees;
- travel or test-centre costs.

**Horizon rule:** build a fresh application budget when the target list is concrete.

### 3.6 Visa procedure is changing rapidly

The PDF describes the China study-visa procedure as of July 2026 and correctly warns that it can change. Federal-level visa digitalization has progressed further: the German Foreign Office now states that study visas can in principle be submitted online through the Consular Services Portal, followed by an in-person biometric / original-document step, while China-specific mission / APS procedures still need to be checked locally.

Official sources:

- Federal Foreign Office Consular Services Portal information: https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/visa-service/215870-215870
- German missions in China — Study visa: https://china.diplo.de/cn-zh/service/visa-einreise/2727522-2727522

**Horizon rule:** for the 2028–2029 cycle, re-check both the Federal Foreign Office portal and the responsible German mission / APS procedure. Do not copy the 2026 submission route forward.

## 4. Important Areas Missing From the PDF for Project Horizon

The PDF is fairly complete **for an admissions manual**, but incomplete **for Horizon's actual Germany MSc route**.

### 4.1 Horizon-specific prerequisite audit

Before target-school construction, create a programme matrix that separately tracks:

- mathematics;
- signals / systems;
- electronics;
- embedded / computer engineering;
- programming / algorithms;
- probability / statistics;
- control / automatic control / modern control;
- robotics-related modules;
- required internship / practical credits;
- thesis / research prerequisites;
- minimum subject-credit categories.

This is especially important because the current undergraduate curriculum has a known possible control-course-chain gap.

### 4.2 Actual evidence profile, not only documents

The future application package should distinguish:

- formal transcript prerequisites;
- GPA / rank;
- deep engineering projects;
- internship evidence;
- research exposure;
- bachelor's thesis;
- technical writing / reproducibility;
- recommendations from people who directly supervised real work.

Projects can prove capability but must not be assumed to replace hard formal credits.

### 4.3 Full all-in cost model

For each serious MSc option, compare:

- tuition;
- semester contribution;
- housing;
- food;
- health insurance;
- transport;
- visa / blocked-account requirements;
- relocation / flights;
- deposits and initial setup;
- emergency buffer;
- scholarships / waivers / guaranteed funding;
- realistic student-work income only as a secondary offset, not as the assumption that makes an otherwise unaffordable route executable.

Current Horizon planning band remains approximately RMB 100,000/year all-in, subject to later reconfirmation.

### 4.4 Housing and city feasibility

A programme can be academically excellent but strategically poor if housing scarcity and cost make the route financially fragile. Munich, Aachen, Berlin and other cities should be compared separately rather than using a single Germany-wide living-cost number.

### 4.5 Scholarships / waivers / funding

The PDF mentions scholarships only lightly. A future target-pool audit should check:

- university tuition waivers;
- DAAD funding;
- Deutschlandstipendium where relevant;
- programme / faculty scholarships;
- foundations;
- research assistant / HiWi opportunities;
- thesis or industry collaboration possibilities.

Only confirmed funding may be counted as executable.

### 4.6 Arrival and residence administration

A complete Horizon route also needs:

- visa → entry;
- Anmeldung / address registration;
- health insurance activation;
- enrollment;
- bank / payment setup;
- residence permit (`Aufenthaltserlaubnis`) after arrival;
- renewal and funding proof;
- tax / social-insurance basics for student jobs.

### 4.7 Student employment and internship strategy

Future planning should distinguish:

- HiWi / research assistant;
- Werkstudent;
- voluntary internship;
- compulsory internship;
- thesis in a company or institute;
- full-time employment before / after graduation where legally possible.

These experiences are not merely income sources. They are evidence-generating experiments for technical fit, work culture, German language and employer access.

### 4.8 Graduation → EU Blue Card → permanent residence

If Germany becomes the chosen route, admissions planning should connect to the post-study system rather than stop at enrollment:

`MSc admission → study + German + internship / Werkstudent → qualified job → preferably EU Blue Card when it is a good fit → permanent residence when eligible`

All salary thresholds, occupation lists, language requirements and residence rules must be reverified in the actual graduation year.

## 5. Safe Use Rules for Horizon

1. The PDF is **reference material**, not authority.
2. Any claim that can affect application eligibility, cost, visa, residence or deadlines must be rechecked against an official current source.
3. Programme-level rules always override generic Germany-wide guidance.
4. Do not treat rankings as the primary selection mechanism; compare programme fit, prerequisites, research / industry ecosystem, cost, mobility and life fit.
5. Do not overfit the undergraduate route to TUM or Germany alone.
6. Preserve course syllabi, contact hours, credits and official training-plan evidence now because curricular matching may later depend on them.
7. Rebuild the actual target pool in the 2028–2029 application cycle using then-current rules.
8. Begin the formal application project roughly 12–18 months before intended enrollment, but do not activate a Graduate execution thread now merely because this reference exists.
9. Treat all 2026 numerical values — tuition, blocked-account amount, application fees, test scores, deadlines and visa processes — as snapshots that require revalidation.
10. When the user provides later Germany guides, merge only verified durable information into this reference; do not accumulate contradictory internet advice without source ranking.
