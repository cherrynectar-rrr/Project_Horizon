It is a strategic document, not an investment-advice feed, trading log, portfolio recommendation or authorization to reallocate execution time. Project Control owns any decision to activate a dedicated Wealth / Investment execution thread.

## Strategic Model — Two Engines

### Engine A — Human Capital and Cash-Flow Creation

Purpose: create the surplus capital that can later compound.

Current examples:

- engineering and research capability;
- strong academic performance and graduate-study optionality;
- international mobility;
- internships and employment;
- career-market positioning;
- communication, judgment and professional reputation;
- entrepreneurship / ownership opportunities if future evidence supports them.

This engine answers:

> How do we increase the amount, resilience and portability of money the user can earn and keep?

### Engine B — Capital Allocation and Compounding

Purpose: turn saved surplus into durable financial assets and future autonomy.

Core capability areas to develop over time:

- personal cash-flow and balance-sheet literacy;
- saving and investable-surplus discipline;
- risk / return / liquidity understanding;
- diversification and asset allocation;
- long-term compounding;
- fees, taxes, inflation and currency risk;
- brokerage / custody / counterparty risk;
- behavioral finance and protection against panic, greed and overconfidence;
- understanding passive investing, active investing and the evidence required to justify either;
- basic valuation and financial-statement literacy when relevant;
- country / tax-residency / legal constraints when the user becomes internationally mobile;
- capital preservation and avoidance of ruin.

This engine answers:

> How do we convert earned surplus into assets that progressively reduce dependence on future labor income?

## Key Strategic Correction

Horizon should **not** frame the long-term choice as `career versus investing`.

The more accurate system is:

> capability / career / ownership → income and investable surplus → disciplined capital allocation → compounding → increasing freedom

Investment is indispensable to the user's desired long-term autonomy, but investment returns multiply an existing capital base. During the undergraduate stage, when financial capital is still small, the marginal value of stronger earning power, better graduate options, avoiding costly mistakes and building correct investing habits can be larger than spending large amounts of time trying to generate exceptional market returns.

Therefore the two engines may be **co-equal strategically while remaining asymmetric in current time allocation**.

This distinction is critical. Treating wealth as a first-order strategic pillar does not require turning undergraduate life into a full-time trading operation.

## Recommended Life-Stage Architecture

### Stage 1 — Undergraduate / Low Financial Capital

Primary wealth objective:

- build earning power and career / geographic optionality;
- establish correct financial habits and investment literacy early;
- avoid large irreversible financial mistakes.

Recommended investment-system role:

- low-time, continuous maintenance;
- learn the structure of markets and instruments;
- learn diversification, risk, liquidity, fees, tax and behavioral principles;
- understand the difference between investing, speculation and trading;
- keep a personal financial record when real income / expenses become meaningful;
- if real-money practice is eventually used, keep it bounded and non-destructive rather than treating tuition, essential living money or borrowed money as investment capital.

### Stage 2 — Early Career / Capital Formation

Primary wealth objective:

- convert rising income into a high-quality investable surplus;
- avoid lifestyle inflation consuming all salary growth;
- build reserves and a robust long-term portfolio architecture;
- understand employer equity, pension / retirement systems, taxes and cross-border constraints where relevant;
- evaluate whether entrepreneurship, equity compensation or ownership creates justified nonlinear upside.

### Stage 3 — Meaningful Capital Base

Primary wealth objective:

- optimize portfolio structure, risk concentration, tax / jurisdiction choices and long-horizon withdrawal / optionality strategy;
- reduce dependence on salary without taking ruin-level risk;
- decide whether active allocation, business ownership, real estate or other concentrated bets deserve a limited role based on evidence and genuine competence.

The phase boundaries should be triggered by real income, savings, capital and life circumstances rather than age alone.

## Wealth Success Metrics

Do not reduce progress to one number such as annual salary or account balance.

Useful future indicators may include:

- earning power and income portability;
- savings / investable-surplus rate;
- liquid safety buffer;
- invested assets;
- diversification and concentration risk;
- months / years of life expenses covered by financial assets;
- dependence on a single employer, country or income source;
- debt burden and downside resilience;
- after-tax / after-fee real returns over meaningful periods;
- increasing ability to say `no` to bad work or take time for travel, learning and family.

The target is **optionality**, not merely a high displayed net worth.

## Non-Negotiable Guardrails

Until Project Control adopts a more detailed policy, Horizon should not confuse wealth-building with high-risk speculation.

Default guardrails:

- no assumption that frequent trading is necessary for financial freedom;
- no guaranteed-return thinking;
- no borrowed-money / leverage strategy merely to accelerate progress;
- no use of tuition, essential living money or emergency funds as speculative capital;
- no concentration justified only by social-media narratives, FOMO or recent price performance;
- no treating one successful trade as evidence of investing skill;
- no ignoring taxes, fees, currency exposure, legal access or custody risk;
- no investment strategy that can plausibly destroy the academic / career / geographic options Horizon is trying to create.

These are risk-governance defaults, not a claim that all active investing or concentrated ownership is always wrong. Larger risks require larger evidence and explicit decision quality.

## Relationship to International Route

Future country / MSc / career analysis should include a wealth-formation dimension in addition to academic and lifestyle fit.

Relevant questions include:

- realistic after-tax disposable income rather than headline salary alone;
- housing and cost-of-living burden;
- ability to save and invest;
- access to diversified financial markets and retirement systems;
- tax treatment of investment income and capital gains;
- currency and remittance constraints;
- immigration stability and ability to change employers;
- entrepreneurship / equity / ownership opportunities;
- whether the location expands or narrows long-term geographic and financial optionality.

This does **not** mean choosing the highest-paying country automatically. The correct target is the best long-term combination of earning power, capital formation, legal stability, health, life quality, mobility and opportunity.

5. Which countries maximize **after-tax investable surplus + market access + life fit**, not merely gross salary?
6. How should the strategy adapt if the user becomes tax-resident in Germany or another country?
7. At what capital / income threshold should wealth work receive materially more execution time?
8. What financial mistakes would create unacceptable downside to graduate study, health, family or mobility?
