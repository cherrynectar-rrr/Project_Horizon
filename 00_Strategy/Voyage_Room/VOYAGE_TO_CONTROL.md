### VTC-20260822-05 — TUM-Primary Multi-School Exam Preparation Strategy

- Date: 2026-08-22
- Type: Graduate-study preparation / future resource-allocation question
- Trigger: After constructing the broader QS Top-100 financially executable target pool, the user explicitly clarified that the broader pool should be retained while **TUM remains the primary target / benchmark**, and asked for the common exam-preparation requirements across all retained schools.
- Related Charter principles: academic optionality; opportunity cost; depth before uncontrolled breadth; international portability; evidence over imagined fit
- Status: **Needs Master Decision — Future Language/Test Sequencing**

### Executive summary

Voyage Room created:

- `00_Strategy/Voyage_Room/MSC_APPLICATION_EXAM_STRATEGY.md`

The current lowest-redundancy test architecture is:

1. **German** — uniquely important because the primary TUM ECE -> Automation & Robotics route currently requires German by the application deadline and is mainly German-taught. Current accepted routes include TestDaF TDN4 in all four sections or telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule.
2. **One English exam only** — recommended common route: IELTS Academic. Planning target: overall 7.0, ideally no component below 6.5. This is a Horizon margin target, not a universal formal minimum. It currently covers the English requirements of Osaka, KAIST, Polimi, SNU, Kyoto, KTH, UTokyo and TUM RCI, while Paris-Saclay requires an English-level certificate without publishing one universal numerical minimum on the programme page.
3. **GRE General — later and conditional.** It is not currently required for TUM ECE, but is required for TUM RCI for China-degree applicants and for the University of Tokyo IME route; Polimi accepts it optionally. If those targets remain in the final pool, a reusable planning target is Quantitative 166+, Analytical Writing 4.0+, Verbal 150+; TUM RCI's current formal minimum is Quant 164 / AW 4.0 with no Verbal minimum.
4. **Common technical/oral interview preparation** should be produced through normal undergraduate capability rather than a separate exam-grinding line: mathematics, circuits/electronics, signals/DSP, control, algorithms/data structures, programming, embedded/sensing, project explanation and research-plan communication.
5. **APS** remains a Germany-specific administrative/verification gate. Current 2026 China procedure may involve APS interview or TestAS depending on the applicable procedure. Because the process is changing, re-check before the real cycle rather than preparing a fixed 2026 workflow now.

### Current programme evidence

- TUM ECE: German required; no current GRE requirement on the programme page; Step-2 applicants may face an aptitude interview.
- TUM RCI: German + English + GRE for applicants whose qualifying degree is from China; current GRE minima Q164 / AW4.0.
- Osaka Engineering Science 21st Century: current English minima TOEFL iBT79 / IELTS6.0 / TOEIC L&R785; overseas screening includes internet interview.
- KAIST international graduate admission: current English minima TOEFL iBT83 / IELTS6.5 / TOEIC720 / TEPS equivalent; Korean is not the general admissions requirement for the selected route.
- Paris-Saclay M1 International Track EE: English programme; certificate of English level compulsory for non-English speakers; no GRE requirement listed on the programme page.
- Polimi Automation and Control: English certificate required (current IELTS6.0 accepted); GRE optional with no general minimum.
- Kyoto Informatics Intelligence Science International Program: TOEFL/TOEIC/IELTS score contributes to selection and current selection includes English research presentation/Q&A.
- SNU international graduate admission: current English route accepts TOEFL iBT80 / IELTS Academic6.0 / TEPS269, or a Korean-language alternative; no general GRE requirement in the international admissions guide.
- University of Tokyo IME: TOEFL/IELTS + GRE General currently required.
- KTH Systems, Control and Robotics: English 6-equivalent requirement; current example IELTS6.5.
- Science Tokyo IGP: current general external English-score requirement has been removed, though scholarship/supervisor may request a score; English is assessed through admission communication/interviews/exams.

### Strategic interpretation

Because TUM remains primary, German has the longest and least substitutable lead time. English is nearly universal across the alternative pool. GRE is narrower and therefore should be delayed until the final pool confirms its value.

Recommended principle:

> **Preserve every exit, but do not prepare every exam at full intensity at the same time.**

The current strategic sequence is:

- German: earliest runway if Control authorizes a language maintenance/support line;
- English: continuous capability, with formal IELTS preparation later;
- technical interview competence: compounded through normal coursework/projects;
- GRE: later, only if RCI / UTokyo / another GRE-requiring target remains alive;
- APS: timed to Germany application procedure.

Japanese, Korean, French and Italian should not be opened as admissions-exam tracks under the current English-track target set. They may later be valuable for life/internships/employment, but that is a separate decision.

### Decision requested from Project Control

Project Control is asked to:

1. acknowledge the user's refinement: broader target pool retained, TUM remains primary target/benchmark;
2. adopt `MSC_APPLICATION_EXAM_STRATEGY.md` as an advisory future preparation map;
3. decide **when**, not merely whether, German should become an active maintenance/support line given its long runway and current TUM dependence;
4. avoid simultaneous full-intensity German + IELTS + GRE preparation while current undergraduate technical/GPA work remains active;
5. defer GRE activation until a later target-pool review confirms TUM RCI / UTokyo or another GRE-requiring programme remains worth the opportunity cost;
6. re-check all test formats and thresholds in the actual 2028–2029 cycle.

### Voyage Room recommendation

**Preserve TUM as primary without overfitting the whole undergraduate route to TUM. Give German the earliest preparation runway; use one reusable English test; keep GRE conditional and later.**

### Confidence

High on the current 2026 programme requirements cited in the exam-strategy document. Medium on the optimal start date/intensity because the early-graduation decision and Project Control workload constraints are not yet resolved. Low on persistence of 2026 thresholds into the actual application cycle; all must be re-verified.
