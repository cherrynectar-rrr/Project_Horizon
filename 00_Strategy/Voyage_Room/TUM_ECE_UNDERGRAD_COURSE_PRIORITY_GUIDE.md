## Important Formal Gaps / Partial Gaps

1. **Systematic control theory** — no required Automatic Control / Modern Control course is visible. Computer Control Technology is the most important available formal bridge, but its syllabus must be preserved for future prerequisite audits.
2. **Solid-state / semiconductor / device physics** — no standalone course is visible. Analog/high-frequency electronics may cover parts, but equivalence must not be assumed.
3. **Numerical Mathematics** — no standalone course visible.
4. **Discrete Mathematics** — no standalone course visible. Current TUM B.Sc. ECE uses Numerics/Discrete as a direction-dependent mathematics choice; do not open a new high-load line solely for the course name.
5. **Stochastic Signals** — no standalone course visible; Probability + Signals + DSP may cover some of the competency.
6. **Electromagnetic field theory** — repairable through the real S5 elective `Electromagnetic Field and Wave`; select it.
7. **Measurement/sensor systems** — repairable through the real S6 elective `Sensors`; select it.
8. Power Engineering is absent but low priority for the user's robotics/control/signal route; do not sacrifice the main route simply to mimic every TUM bachelor module.

## How to Study the S/S+ Courses

For each S/S+ course, keep:
- official syllabus/course description;
- credit/contact-hour evidence;
- textbook table of contents;
- labs/assignments/course design reports;
- a 1–2 page English summary of what was learned and what was implemented.

TUM currently states that a transcript with course titles alone is insufficient for course-guide review; module content and acquired competencies must be visible.

## TUM Current Admission Reference

Current 2024 FPSO Annex 6 uses a competence-based curricular analysis:
- Higher Mathematics: max 32 points
- Physics Fundamentals: max 15 points
- EE/IT/CS: max 23 points
- Curricular total: 70 points
- Grade component: max 30 points
- Stage-1 total >=75: pass
- 60–74: interview
- <60: fail

The curricular analysis is explicitly based on competencies/learning outcomes, not a mechanical course-name comparison.
