# RWTH Aachen Robotics-Aligned MSc Route Reference

Last Updated: 2026-08-22
Owner: Voyage Room
Status: Strategic reference; current-policy snapshot only

## Purpose

Record the current 2026 RWTH Aachen MSc route that best combines the user's Electronic Information Engineering curriculum with the long-term robotics / intelligent-systems direction.

## Best Current Structural Match

**RWTH Aachen M.Sc. Electrical Engineering and Information Technology → Systems and Automation (SYAT)**

Why it matters:
- The public EEIT MSc is the natural continuation of an EE/ECE-like bachelor rather than a mechanical-heavy robotics conversion.
- The SYAT track is explicitly centered on systems, modelling, control, optimisation and automation, with direct relevance to robotics and autonomous technical systems.
- This preserves the user's electronics, signal-processing, embedded and computing foundation while moving upward into control / automation.

## Current 2026 Language Rule

- The current EEIT MSc is taught in English.
- The current regulations require English proficiency under RWTH §3(9) rules.
- Current accepted examples include TOEFL iBT 90, IELTS 6.0, Cambridge B2 First grade B or higher, PTE Academic 60, UNIcert II or equivalent B2 evidence.
- German is not currently listed as an admission-language requirement for this EEIT MSc.

This differs materially from the current TUM ECE route, where German is a formal admissions requirement.

## Current 2026 GRE Rule

The current EEIT MSc regulations require a GRE General Test for applicants unless they fall under an exemption such as EU/EEA citizenship or German educational-background exceptions.

Current threshold is expressed by percentile:
- Quantitative Reasoning: at least the 85th percentile (top 15%).
- Verbal Reasoning: at least the 15th percentile.

Do not convert these percentile requirements into a permanent fixed numeric GRE score because percentile mappings can change.

## Current Subject-Prerequisite Architecture

The current regulations compare prior coursework to RWTH's EEIT bachelor and require subject coverage including approximately:
- 28 CP higher mathematics;
- 10 CP physics / physical foundations of electrical engineering;
- 34 CP theoretical electrical-engineering foundations including circuits;
- 12 CP computer-science / programming fundamentals;
- 8 CP systems-theory foundations;
- 8 CP theoretical specialisation in EE or CS;
- 20 CP application-oriented coursework in areas such as circuits, microelectronics, communications, energy engineering, technical computer science or medical engineering.

Conditional admission may be possible with limited missing coursework, but the current regulations impose limits on how many additional requirements may be assigned.

For Horizon, the user's likely strengths are mathematics, circuits/electronics, signals/DSP, embedded systems, communications and programming. The main audit risk is the formal **systems-theory / control** block, which must later be mapped carefully against `Signals and Systems`, `Computer Control Technology` and other transcripted coursework.

## Why Not Default to RWTH Robotic Systems Engineering

RWTH International Academy's current M.Sc. Robotic Systems Engineering is a continuing-education/private programme with additional tuition, work-experience requirements and a prerequisite architecture benchmarked strongly against Mechanical Engineering, including mechanics, machine design, materials, thermodynamics and manufacturing. It is therefore currently a weaker curriculum match for the user's Electronic Information Engineering background despite the attractive programme name.

## Current TUM vs RWTH Trade-off

Under 2026 rules:

- **TUM ECE → Automation & Robotics:** very high curriculum fit; German required; no GRE currently listed.
- **RWTH EEIT → Systems & Automation:** very high curriculum fit; English-taught; no German currently required for admission; GRE required for the user's likely applicant category.

Therefore language burden and GRE burden trade places between the two routes. This comparison is strategic only and must be rechecked in the actual 2028–2029 application cycle.
