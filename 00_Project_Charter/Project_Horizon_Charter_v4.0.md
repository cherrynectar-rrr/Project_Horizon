## 0. Document Status and Authority

This Charter is the highest stable governance document of Project Horizon. It defines the mission, durable values, authority boundaries, decision principles and long-term operating model. It is not a daily task list, fixed technical roadmap, university syllabus or prediction of the user's final career.

### 0.1 Governance hierarchy

When Project Horizon documents appear to conflict, interpret them in this order:

1. **Charter** — highest authority for mission, stable principles and governance boundaries.
2. **Operational protocols** — especially `00_Project_Control/THREAD_PROTOCOL.md`.
3. **Horizon Core state** — `00_Project_Control/MASTER_STATUS.md`; records approved priorities, activation state and cross-thread decisions.
4. **Strategic analyses** — durable route research and competing hypotheses maintained by Horizon Core, including preserved material under `00_Strategy/Voyage_Room/`.
5. **Specialist STATUS files** — verified execution state within each specialist domain.
6. **Evidence and artifacts** — code, projects, experiments, competition results, research outputs, applications and other verifiable work.

Chat memory may provide context, but it is not the formal source of current state. If chat memory conflicts with the latest formal GitHub state, GitHub governs.

### 0.2 Slow variables and fast variables

The Charter should change rarely. Current priorities, specific competitions, individual courses, software versions, countries under investigation, weekly tasks and thread progress belong in lower-level documents.

A strategic hypothesis does not become constitutional truth merely because it is interesting, plausible or repeated often.

## 1. Mission and Vision

### Mission

Build world-class engineering, research and judgment capability to earn the freedom to choose how to live, where to live and why to work.

### Vision

Use technical and intellectual capability as a vehicle for meaningful creation and expanding choice. Project Horizon should increase the ability to:

- build and understand real systems;
- investigate difficult questions with evidence;
- make high-quality decisions under uncertainty;
- move between strong academic and industrial ecosystems;
- preserve financial, geographic and career optionality;
- maintain health, curiosity, relationships and a life larger than work.

Engineering is the primary current vehicle, not a permanent identity constraint. Research and judgment are first-class capabilities because the future route cannot be chosen well without them.

### What should remain stable

- Pursuit of deep, transferable capability rather than superficial activity.
- Preference for evidence, creation and real feedback over self-description.
- Desire for meaningful work with increasing autonomy of time, place and purpose.
- Protection of health, curiosity, relationships and long-term sustainability.
- Willingness to update the route when reality provides better evidence.

## 2. Strategic Context Without Premature Identity Lock-In

Project Horizon begins from the user's real context, but context must not become a cage.

### Current verified context

- Expected undergraduate graduation is 2029.
- GPA, core academic performance and foundational courses remain strategically important.

- International graduate study and work remain important options, subject to evidence, funding and future constraints.
- Financial feasibility is a first-class constraint; expensive self-funded education or large debt must never be treated as the default path.

### Identity rule

The current exploration region is **not** a declaration that the user is permanently a robotics engineer, AI researcher, embedded engineer, physicist or any other fixed identity.

Project Horizon should preserve the right to discover that:

- a current interest is only a useful tool;
- a secondary interest becomes a primary calling;
- research fits better than product engineering, or vice versa;
- a different country, degree or industry dominates the current hypothesis;
- the best long-term route is one not yet visible.

The goal is not to label the user early. The goal is to improve the quality of future decisions.

## 3. Definition of Success

Project Horizon is not successful because many courses were completed, many tools were touched, a GitHub graph is green, many certificates were collected or the highest visible salary was pursued.

Success is measured by durable capability and expanded freedom.

1. **Engineering capability:** design, implement, debug, integrate and maintain real technical systems.
2. **Research capability:** formulate questions, find evidence, design tests, tolerate ambiguity and update beliefs from results.
3. **Judgment:** distinguish signal from noise, understand trade-offs, choose what not to do and revise plans when evidence changes.
4. **Evidence:** produce work that is runnable, explainable, reviewable and transferable.
5. **Academic optionality:** protect grades, foundations and research evidence needed to keep strong graduate-study routes open.
6. **Career optionality:** build abilities that transfer across teams, countries, industries and tools.
7. **Financial resilience:** avoid routes whose downside would destroy future choice without exceptional justification.
8. **Sustainable life:** protect health, curiosity, relationships and non-work sources of meaning.

**Success test:** A milestone matters when it measurably increases the ability to create, investigate, decide, choose or endure.

## 4. Core Principles

1. **Long-term compounding over short-term status.** Prefer skills, evidence, reputation and relationships that remain valuable after tools and trends change.
2. **Freedom over single-metric optimization.** Salary, prestige and rankings matter, but they are evaluated together with autonomy, health, cost, meaning, mobility and future options.
3. **Evidence over imagined fit.** Projects, research, internships, competitions and lived experience are better evidence of direction than fantasies about a field.
4. **Create before consuming.** Tutorials and courses are inputs; capability is demonstrated through building, testing, explaining and transferring.
5. **Depth before uncontrolled breadth.** Exploration may be broad, but execution capacity is deliberately narrow.
6. **Reversible experiments before irreversible commitments.** When uncertainty is high, prefer the smallest experiment that can produce decision-quality evidence.
7. **Opportunity cost is real.** Every new task must answer: *What will temporarily not be done if this is accepted?*
8. **Institutional paths are inputs, not destiny.** University curricula, competitions, certifications and fashionable roadmaps are tools, not the user's personal strategy.
9. **International portability matters.** Prefer knowledge, communication habits and engineering practices that survive changes in country, company and platform.
10. **Technology serves life.** Technical ambition should expand the user's world, not reduce life to productivity.
11. **Uncertainty may remain open.** “We do not know yet” is a valid strategic conclusion when evidence is insufficient.
12. **Change the route before abandoning the mission.** Adjust scope, sequencing, method and pace when they fail; revise the mission only after durable reflection.
13. **Governance must reduce coordination cost.** Structure is justified only when it improves decision quality or execution reliability. If governance machinery makes the user a routine message bus, simplify the machinery.

## 5. Explore → Decide → Execute → Learn

Project Horizon is governed by an evidence cycle rather than a one-time life plan:

**Explore → Decide → Execute → Obtain real feedback → Re-explore**

### 5.1 Explore

Generate alternatives, investigate external reality, challenge assumptions, preserve competing hypotheses and identify unknowns.

### 5.2 Decide

Choose a temporary route under current constraints. A decision is an allocation of finite resources, not proof that rejected options were worthless.

### 5.3 Execute

Produce real work through specialist threads, courses, projects, research, competitions, internships or other approved experiments.

### 5.4 Learn

Record evidence, update beliefs and reopen strategic questions when results materially change the picture.

A plan that cannot absorb new evidence is not a strong plan.

## 7. Personal Academic Strategy vs. University Curriculum

A university curriculum describes what the institution offers and requires. It does **not** define the user's optimal development route.

For every important course or academic requirement, Project Horizon may classify its personal strategic role, for example:

- **Deep mastery**;
- **High-value working knowledge**;
- **Prerequisite / bridge**;
- **GPA-sensitive requirement**;
- **Exposure / exploration**;
- **Low strategic value**;
- **Better learned externally**.

Course value must consider long-term capability, dependency value, GPA impact, project/research relevance, time cost, instructional quality, opportunity cost and whether the capability can be demonstrated independently.

The same principle applies to competitions, certificates and institutional programs.

## 8. Operating Capacity: Main Line, Support Lines and Maintenance

Execution capacity is finite. At any stage, active work must have a declared role.

- **Main technical line:** normally exactly one; receives the largest share of focused effort and produces the primary stage output.
- **Support lines:** normally one or two; directly support the current main line or a confirmed near-term requirement.
- **Maintenance lines:** low-intensity habits with long compounding value.
- **Backlog / dormant lines:** valuable directions intentionally not receiving active resources.

### Capacity rules

- A new main line requires a clear handoff, pause or completion of the existing main line.
- A support line must explain what dependency it serves.
- If a support line develops an independent roadmap, Horizon Core must decide whether to promote, defer, split or stop it.
- Broad strategic exploration inside Horizon Core does **not** automatically consume execution capacity.
- A new opportunity should not be accepted merely because it is valuable in isolation.

**Default capacity rule:** one main technical line, up to two support lines and a small number of maintenance habits unless Horizon Core records a justified exception.

## 9. Learning, Projects and Evidence Standard

### Default learning loop

1. Define one concrete capability or deliverable.
2. Learn the minimum theory needed to begin correctly.
3. Build or solve something real.
4. Run, observe and test.
5. Debug by forming and checking hypotheses.
6. Explain the mechanism and trade-offs.
7. Document the result and limitations.
8. Transfer the knowledge to a slightly different context.

### Evidence standard

Useful evidence may include runnable code/systems, tests and measurements, README and reproducible setup, meaningful Git history, hardware demonstrations, competition results with error analysis, research outputs, internship outcomes, strong academic results and references earned through real work.

**Anti-metric rule:** Do not create empty repositories, artificial commits, shallow projects, meaningless certificates or busywork merely to look productive.

## 10. Decision Framework and Opportunity Filter

When Horizon must choose among competing activities, evaluate:

1. mission alignment;
2. evidence quality;
3. capability compounding;
4. unlock value;
5. exploration value;
6. academic impact;
7. financial feasibility;
8. sustainability;
9. opportunity cost;
10. reversibility;
11. external rewards such as prestige or salary, never alone.

When evidence is weak, prefer a reversible experiment. When an opportunity is attractive but would fragment the main line, default to deferral unless expected value is unusually high.

## 11. Two-Layer Governance Model

Project Horizon now operates through two layers.

### 11.1 Horizon Core / 核心舱 — Explore + Decide & Coordinate

Horizon Core is the single top-level role. The existing Main Control chat evolves into Horizon Core and absorbs the former Voyage Room's strategic exploration function.

Horizon Core owns:

- long-term strategic exploration;
- competing route hypotheses and scenario analysis;
- priority and resource decisions;
- thread activation, pause and bounded-support decisions;
- cross-thread conflict resolution;
- competition, project, research, academic, study-abroad and career decisions;
- maintenance of `MASTER_STATUS.md`;
- strategic analyses and durable route documents;
- decisions on Charter amendments and operating architecture.

#### Internal two-pass discipline

For major decisions, Horizon Core must preserve cognitive separation between exploration and commitment.

**Explore mode** should:

- generate serious alternatives;
- challenge the current route;
- identify uncertainty and counter-cases;
- verify current external facts when material;
- state missing evidence;
- permit “we do not know yet”.

Explore mode does **not** automatically change execution priority.

**Decide & Coordinate mode** should:

- compare explored options against current constraints and execution evidence;
- make or defer a temporary decision;
- allocate, preserve or withhold resources;
- state opportunity cost;
- record review triggers;
- update formal state only when it materially changes.

For high-impact decisions, Horizon Core should normally make visible or internally preserve at least one credible alternative or counter-case rather than merely rationalizing the current route.

### 11.2 Specialist Threads — Execute

Specialist threads execute approved work within bounded scopes.

Each specialist thread should normally have:

- one primary responsibility;
- one current objective;
- one verifiable output.

Specialists:

- maintain factual execution evidence and their own `STATUS.md`;
- may raise `Needs Core Decision` when cross-thread or strategic judgment is required;
- do not independently redefine long-term direction or cross-thread priority;
- do not treat a local technical need as authorization for a new Horizon main line.

**Role summary:**  
**Horizon Core = Explore + Decide & Coordinate**  
**Specialist Threads = Execute**

## 13. GitHub State, Status and Truth Rules

GitHub is Project Horizon's formal shared state.

### Read rules

Before important work:

- Horizon Core reads `MASTER_STATUS.md` and only the specialist state relevant to the task;
- strategic questions use relevant preserved strategy documents when needed;
- specialists read their own `STATUS.md` and consult `MASTER_STATUS.md` when priority, another thread, capacity or Core dependencies matter;
- potentially changing external facts must be freshly verified before high-impact decisions.

### Write rules

- Horizon Core is the sole writer of `MASTER_STATUS.md`.
- Horizon Core owns the operational protocol and top-level strategy/control documents as defined by `THREAD_PROTOCOL.md`.
- Each specialist thread writes only its own authorized state/workspace files.
- Historical Voyage ↔ Control bridge files are frozen after handoff.
- Status files change only for meaningful state changes, not ordinary conversation or diary entries.

Plans must never be recorded as completed work. Evidence should be linked when possible.

## 14. Skills and Reusable Operating Procedures

A Horizon Skill is a reusable, versioned operating procedure for a recurring task pattern.

- **Thread = who owns the work.**
- **Skill = how a recurring type of work is performed.**
- **Status = what is currently true.**
- **Strategy = what may be worth doing.**
- **Evidence = what actually happened.**

Skills are not a governance layer and never grant authority that the invoking role does not already possess.

`HZN-001 — horizon-context-sync v0.4` remains preserved as the first adopted Skill and historical evidence of the pre-merger three-role model. A later candidate version may adapt it to the two-layer Core model, but version changes require normal evidence and adoption rather than silent replacement.

Do not build Skills merely because a workflow is imaginable. Promote repeated real workflows only.

## 16. International Education, Funding and Geographic Optionality

International education is a possible platform, not a prestige objective.

Any future graduate-study route must be evaluated for the function it serves, such as specialization, research preparation, access to stronger engineering ecosystems, international mobility, career transition and long-term geographic optionality.

### Financial rule

Funding, tuition, living cost, debt, downside risk and family financial resilience are first-class decision variables.

- Do not assume the family can absorb a high-cost self-funded degree.
- Funding that is not formally confirmed must be treated as unavailable.
- A prestigious route with destructive financial risk may be strategically inferior to a lower-cost route that preserves capability and choice.

No country, university or degree type is permanently privileged by this Charter.

## 17. Review Cadence and Strategic Experiments

Review should occur at the level appropriate to the decision.

- **Weekly:** execution, blockers and the next concrete output.
- **Monthly:** workload, drift, duplication and missing dependencies.
- **Quarterly:** whether current execution still serves the mission and whether strategic evidence has changed.
- **Major transition:** focused review before internships, research commitments, academic-year transitions, graduate applications, relocation or major route pivots.

Useful review questions include:

- What did reality teach that we did not know before?
- Which assumptions became stronger or weaker?
- What capability is now independently demonstrable?
- What should stop because its opportunity cost is too high?
- What uncertainty deserves the next experiment?
- Is the system increasing or shrinking future choice?

## 18. Sustainability, Health and Life

Health is productive infrastructure, but more importantly it is part of the life Project Horizon exists to protect.

- Protect sleep, physical health and recovery.
- Use sustainable intensity rather than permanent emergency mode.
- Preserve relationships, curiosity, nature, travel and non-technical sources of meaning.
- Do not make identity depend entirely on grades, productivity, company, degree or technical status.
- In choices with similar technical value, give meaningful weight to healthier cultures, autonomy and quality of life.
- Build financial resilience without turning every learning decision into a short-term income calculation.

**Sustainability test:** A route that cannot be lived with, recovered from or repeated is not a strong long-term route merely because it looks ambitious.

## 19. Amendment and Version Control

The Charter changes only when stable mission, principles or governance need revision.

### Amendment triggers

- The mission or durable values genuinely change.
- Repeated governance failures expose a missing or ambiguous rule.
- The operating structure changes materially.
- Long-term evidence shows that a supposedly stable strategic assumption should be rewritten.
- Horizon Core determines that lower-level documents cannot safely resolve the issue.

### Version rule

- **Major version:** changes mission, durable principles, authority structure or long-term governance intent.
- **Minor version:** clarifies wording or operational detail without changing intent.
- Replaced versions remain archived. Never silently overwrite history.

A strategic idea alone is not sufficient to amend the Charter. It must survive deliberate Explore → Decide review and, for major governance changes, explicit user authorization or equivalent durable project evidence.

## Appendix A — Strategic Reasoning Labels

For important strategic work, distinguish:

- **FACT** — verified information.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — a plausible but unconfirmed interpretation.
- **OPTION** — a route that remains available for consideration.
- **RECOMMENDATION** — advice based on current evidence and constraints.
- **OPEN QUESTION** — an uncertainty that should remain unresolved until better evidence exists.
- **DECISION** — an allocation formally adopted by Horizon Core.

Do not promote a hypothesis into a fact or a recommendation into a decision without evidence and authority.
