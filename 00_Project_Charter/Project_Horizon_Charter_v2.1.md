## 0. Document Status and Scope

This Charter is the stable source of truth for Project Horizon's mission, values, decision rules, learning governance and long-term direction. It is not a daily task list, syllabus or fixed timetable.

### Document hierarchy

- **Charter:** stable mission, principles, governance and decision rules.
- **Roadmap:** adaptive 6–12 month direction, milestones and sequencing.
- **Quarterly / Monthly Review:** priority changes, capacity checks and route calibration.
- **Weekly Review:** execution, progress, blockers and next actions.
- **Domain README / Chat Thread:** concrete teaching, projects, notes and evidence for one responsibility.

When documents conflict, the Charter governs the mission and principles; the most recent approved Roadmap governs current sequencing; the latest review governs near-term execution.

## 1. Mission and Vision

### Mission

Build world-class engineering ability to earn the freedom to choose how and where to live.

### Vision

Use technology as a tool to create a life with freedom of time, place and purpose. Work because I choose to, not because I have to.

Project Horizon exists to connect technical growth, meaningful creation and life design. Engineering is the vehicle; freedom, contribution, curiosity and a well-lived life are the destination.

### What must remain stable

- The pursuit of deep, transferable engineering ability.
- The goal of meaningful work with increasing autonomy of time and place.
- The commitment to build rather than only consume.
- The belief that technology must serve life, not replace it.

## 3. Definition of Success

Project Horizon is not successful merely because many courses were completed, many tools were touched, the GitHub graph is green, or the highest available salary was obtained. Success is measured by durable capability and expanded freedom.

1. Build the ability to design, implement, debug and maintain real technical systems.
2. Produce evidence that is runnable, explainable, reviewable and transferable to new contexts.
3. Become capable of entering and growing within robotics, embodied AI and AI systems work.
4. Preserve the ability to choose among countries, industries, teams and ways of living.
5. Develop judgment: know what to learn, what to ignore, when to persist and when to adjust.
6. Sustain health, curiosity, relationships and a life larger than work.
7. Build financial resilience and, over time, meaningful independence without abandoning meaningful creation.

**Success test:** A milestone matters when it increases the ability to create, explain, choose or endure — preferably more than one at the same time.

## 4. Core Principles

1. **Long-term thinking over short-term gains.** Prefer compounding skills, reputation and optionality over temporary status or fast but fragile rewards.
2. **Freedom is more important than maximizing salary.** Income matters, but it is evaluated together with autonomy, health, meaning, mobility and future options.
3. **Engineering ability is built through projects.** Knowledge becomes capability only through implementation, debugging, integration and reflection.
4. **Create before consuming.** Prefer making, testing and explaining over endless tutorials, feeds and passive collection.
5. **Depth before uncontrolled breadth.** Learn enough adjacent material to support the active main line; do not open every attractive direction at once.
6. **Evidence over self-description.** Replace “I know this” with runnable code, a clear explanation, a documented design or a solved problem.
7. **Treat setbacks as route adjustments, not reasons to quit.** Debug the plan, scope or method before abandoning the mission.
8. **International portability matters.** Prefer concepts, tools and communication habits that transfer across teams, countries and platforms.
9. **Stay curious about the world, nature and the universe.** Technical ambition should expand life, not narrow it.
10. **Technology serves life; life does not exist only for work.** Protect the human purpose behind the technical system.

## 5. Long-Term Technical Direction

**Technical North Star:** Build toward the ability to create intelligent systems that perceive, reason and act in the physical world — from embedded foundations to robot software, AI models and accelerated deployment.

### Capability stack

- **Engineering foundations:** Python, C++, Linux, Git, debugging, software design and technical communication.
- **Computer science foundations:** Data structures, algorithms, complexity, systems thinking and networking basics.
- **Hardware–software foundations:** Embedded systems, electronics context, sensors, actuators, communication protocols and real-time concepts.
- **Robotics systems:** ROS2, simulation, perception, localization, planning, control and system integration.
- **AI perception and learning:** Computer vision, machine learning, deep learning and model evaluation.
- **Deployment and acceleration:** Inference systems, profiling, CUDA, TensorRT or equivalent acceleration concepts, and edge deployment.
- **Embodied AI integration:** Combine perception, reasoning, control, hardware and deployment into reliable physical agents.

### Default sequence

**Python → C++ → Linux → Data Structures & Algorithms → Embedded → ROS2 → Computer Vision → Deep Learning → CUDA / Acceleration → Embodied AI**

This is a dependency-guided sequence, not a rigid law. Topics may overlap when a real project creates a clear need, but overlap must not become uncontrolled parallelism.

## 6. Operating Model: Main Line, Support Lines and Maintenance

At any stage, every learning activity must have a declared role.

- **Main technical line:** exactly one; produces the primary stage project and receives the largest share of focused effort.
- **Support lines:** usually one or two; provide capabilities directly required by the main line and do not expand without approval.
- **Maintenance lines:** low-intensity habits that compound over time, such as English, review, reading or physical health.
- **Backlog / dormant lines:** valuable directions intentionally deferred until prerequisites, capacity or strategic timing improve.

### Activation rules

- A new main line may begin only after the current stage reaches a clear handoff, completion or deliberate pause.
- A support line must state which current project or dependency it serves.
- When a support line starts generating its own independent roadmap, it must either become the main line, be split into its own approved phase, or return to the backlog.
- The total active workload must fit real available time and energy; ambition does not override capacity.

**Default capacity rule:** One main technical line, up to two support lines and a small number of maintenance habits. More is an exception that requires a clear reason and review date.

## 7. Learning and Creation Standard

### Default learning loop

1. **Goal:** define one concrete capability or deliverable.
2. **Minimum theory:** learn only the concepts needed to begin correctly.
3. **Build:** implement a small but real task.
4. **Inspect:** run, test, observe and compare with expected behavior.
5. **Debug:** trace errors, form hypotheses and fix the underlying cause.
6. **Explain:** describe how the solution works and why key choices were made.
7. **Document:** record usage, lessons, limitations and next steps.
8. **Transfer:** apply the concept in a slightly different context or project.

### Teaching and self-study rules

- Prefer small, complete steps over large, vague assignments.
- Attempt first; receive hints before full solutions unless blocked by missing prerequisite knowledge.
- Explain errors by cause, execution path and repair principle — not only by replacement code.
- Use experiments to verify important concepts.
- Review old knowledge inside new projects instead of relying only on isolated repetition.
- Avoid tutorial accumulation. A tutorial is useful only when it unlocks a build, decision or explanation.

## 8. Project and Evidence Standard

### A good Project Horizon project

- Solves a defined problem or demonstrates a defined system behavior.
- Runs reliably enough for its current scope.
- Is slightly beyond current comfort but small enough to finish.
- Contains understandable structure, naming and documentation.
- Includes a way to reproduce, run or test the result.
- Demonstrates at least one newly acquired capability.
- Can evolve into a stronger project or teach a transferable pattern.
- Whenever practical, connects to robotics, embedded systems, AI systems or engineering tooling.

### Minimum evidence package

- **Runnable artifact:** source code, configuration, data or hardware instructions needed for the current scope.
- **README:** purpose, setup, run steps, features, structure and limitations.
- **Learning record:** what was learned, what failed, what remains unclear and what comes next.
- **Version history:** meaningful commits that reflect coherent progress, not artificial activity.
- **Demonstration:** terminal output, test result, screenshot, video or concise walkthrough when appropriate.

**Anti-metric rule:** Do not create empty repositories, mechanical commits, copied projects without understanding, or artificial activity merely to make progress look impressive.

## 9. Capability Levels and Definition of Done

- **Exposed:** recognize the idea and follow a guided example.
- **Understood:** explain the core mechanism and predict basic behavior.
- **Independent:** complete a standard task without copying a full solution.
- **Transferable:** use the idea in a new context and select it appropriately.
- **Engineering-ready:** design, debug, test, document, maintain and explain trade-offs in a real system.

### Project Horizon Definition of Done

A learning stage is complete only when the evidence appropriate to that stage shows most of the following:

1. I can explain the central concepts in my own words.
2. I can implement the required behavior without reproducing a complete reference solution.
3. I can diagnose common failures and use tools or experiments to investigate unfamiliar ones.
4. I have completed a runnable project, feature or problem set that demonstrates the target capability.
5. I have documented how to run it, what I learned and what remains limited.
6. I can reuse the knowledge in a slightly different problem.

“Covered” is not the same as “complete.” Completion is based on demonstrated capability, not exposure time.

## 10. Decision Framework and Opportunity Filter

Evaluate competing options in this order:

1. **Mission alignment:** does it support the long-term life and engineering mission?
2. **Core capability:** does it deepen a capability that compounds across future stages?
3. **Real evidence:** can it produce a meaningful, verifiable result?
4. **Unlock value:** does it open the next dependency, project, role or international option?
5. **Transferability:** will the learning remain valuable across tools, companies and countries?
6. **Sustainability:** can it fit health, time, finances and real energy?
7. **Opportunity cost:** what important work must be delayed or abandoned?
8. **External rewards:** salary, prestige, certificates and trend value are considered last, not ignored.

### Default decision rules

- Prefer opportunities that strengthen several criteria at once.
- A fashionable option that weakens the main line and produces little evidence should normally be deferred.
- A difficult option may still be correct when it builds a critical dependency and remains sustainable.
- When information is weak, run a small reversible experiment before making a large irreversible commitment.
- Do not change direction primarily because of temporary frustration, online comparison or fear of being behind.

## 11. Layered Governance and Multi-Thread Model

Project Horizon has three distinct layers. They cooperate through a clear decision flow, but they are not peer execution threads.

### Strategic layer — Voyage Room / 远航室

- Explores long-horizon questions about technical direction, education, career, international options, life design and major opportunities.
- Develops scenarios, assumptions, trade-offs, risks and recommendations.
- Maintains strategic context in `00_Strategy/Voyage_Room/`.
- May challenge current assumptions and propose route changes to Project Control.
- Does not allocate resources, activate execution lines, edit specialist status files or treat a recommendation as an approved decision.
- Is not a learning or delivery thread and is not parallel to Python, C++, Linux, Algorithm or other execution domains.

### Control layer — Project Control

- Maintains the mission, current priorities and cross-thread operating model.
- Synthesizes Voyage Room recommendations with execution evidence and real constraints.
- Approves activation, pause, merge or split of execution threads.
- Allocates attention and resources across the active main, support and maintenance lines.
- Resolves cross-domain conflicts and owns `00_Project_Control/MASTER_STATUS.md`.
- Decides when a Roadmap or Charter amendment should be proposed or adopted.

### Execution layer — Domain threads

- Python, C++, Linux, Algorithm and other specialist domains execute concrete learning, projects and evidence production.
- Each domain has one primary responsibility and works within its approved scope.
- Each reports progress, blockers and handoff requirements in its own `STATUS.md`.
- No execution thread independently creates a new long-term main line or redefines global priorities.
- Cross-domain, time-allocation, education, career and life-strategy decisions return to Project Control.

### Decision flow

1. The Voyage Room explores and makes a documented recommendation.
2. Project Control combines that recommendation with current status, capacity and evidence.
3. Project Control records the decision and allocates or withholds resources.
4. Execution threads implement approved work and return factual evidence.
5. New evidence may reopen a strategic question without automatically reversing the decision.

### Strategic communication layer

The Voyage Room and Project Control communicate through two single-writer files:

- `00_Strategy/Voyage_Room/VOYAGE_TO_CONTROL.md` is maintained only by the Voyage Room. Project Control and execution threads read it but do not edit it.
- `00_Project_Control/CONTROL_TO_VOYAGE.md` is maintained only by Project Control. The Voyage Room and execution threads read it but do not edit it.

The Voyage Room updates its channel when it develops a meaningful long-term judgment, route hypothesis, major recommendation, risk finding or question requiring control-level judgment. Before deciding long-term direction, academic or international routes, career or research direction, major competitions or projects, resource reallocation or an overall route adjustment, Project Control reads the latest Voyage Room transmission.

Project Control evaluates recommendations against `MASTER_STATUS.md`, relevant specialist `STATUS.md` files, Opportunity Radar facts and current real-world constraints. It records important feedback, adopted decisions and further research requests in its outbound channel.

Before strategic analysis that depends on Project Horizon's real state, the Voyage Room reads `MASTER_STATUS.md`, `CONTROL_TO_VOYAGE.md` and any necessary specialist status files.

The Voyage Room has broad exploratory freedom. It may challenge the current route, hold competing hypotheses and study very long-term questions; it is not constrained by execution-thread rules such as one current deliverable. This freedom does not grant authority to schedule execution threads, allocate resources or edit `MASTER_STATUS.md`.

**Role summary:** Voyage Room = Explore; Main Control = Decide & Coordinate; Specialist Threads = Execute.

### Thread lifecycle

- **Create:** when a responsibility becomes active, recurring and distinct enough to benefit from dedicated context.
- **Keep combined:** while one subject mainly supports another and independent context remains small.
- **Split:** when responsibilities, deliverables, vocabularies and review cycles become independently substantial or history becomes hard to navigate.
- **Pause:** when prerequisites, capacity or strategic timing are missing.
- **Close / archive:** when the objective is complete, obsolete or absorbed into another thread; preserve final status and handoff notes.

**Responsibility test:** If a thread cannot state one clear responsibility, one current objective and one expected output, its scope is too broad or it should not yet exist.

## 12. Review Cadence and Route Adjustment

- **Weekly:** execution, evidence, blockers and the next smallest meaningful step.
- **Monthly:** alignment, realistic workload, duplication, drift and missing foundations.
- **Quarterly:** whether the main line still fits the mission, opportunities and evidence; what should start, stop, continue or change.
- **Major transition:** focused strategic review before internships, academic-year changes, graduate application stages, relocation or major project pivots.

### Valid reasons for major adjustment

- The long-term goal has genuinely changed after reflection.
- New reliable information materially changes the expected path.
- Sustained practical experience shows the current path is unsuitable or inefficient.
- A clearly better opportunity appears and aligns with the mission.
- Health, finances, family or other real constraints change.

### Invalid reasons on their own

- A short period of frustration or slow progress.
- A new trend, tool or influencer creates fear of missing out.
- Comparison with someone on a different timeline.
- The desire to avoid the discomfort of fundamentals.

When adjusting, change the scope, order, pace or method before changing the mission.

## 13. Sustainability, Health and Life

- Protect sleep, physical health and recovery as productive infrastructure, not optional rewards.
- Use sustainable intensity: periods of focused effort should be followed by recovery and review.
- Do not treat rest as failure or a deviation from the plan.
- Preserve relationships, curiosity, nature, exploration and non-technical sources of meaning.
- In choices with similar technical value, prefer healthier cultures, greater autonomy and stronger alignment with personal values.
- Avoid making identity depend entirely on productivity, grades, job title or technical status.
- Build financial resilience gradually, without turning every learning decision into a short-term income calculation.

**Sustainability test:** A plan that cannot be repeated, recovered from or lived with is not a strong long-term plan, even when it looks ambitious for one week.

## 14. Amendment and Version Control

The Charter should change rarely. Detailed schedules, courses, tools, project backlogs and weekly targets belong in lower-level documents and may change frequently.

### Amendment triggers

- A stable mission or core value has genuinely changed.
- A repeated governance problem shows that a rule is missing or ambiguous.
- The long-term technical direction changes in a durable way.
- The operating model no longer fits the real structure of Project Horizon.
- A quarterly review identifies a change important enough to govern future decisions.

### Amendment process

1. State the problem and why lower-level documents cannot solve it.
2. Identify the exact sections affected.
3. Preserve stable language unless change is necessary.
4. Record the rationale and effective date.
5. Increase the major version for changes to mission, principles or governance; increase the minor version for clarifications that do not change intent.
6. Archive the replaced version; do not silently overwrite history.
